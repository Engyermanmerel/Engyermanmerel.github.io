Dynamic Seismic Validation of a Diesel Engine Support Structure

Introduction

This Dynamic analysis is part of the design of the structural steel support system with a reinforced slab to support more than two industrial motors by Caterpillar. These motors are responsible for generating electricity for an industrial plant in the province of Veraguas, Panama. The purpose is to evaluate the integrity of the structure under vibrations induced by a natural phenomenon such as an earthquake with a magnitude of 8 on the Richter scale, since that region has been affected more than once over the years by earthquakes of this magnitude. The growing concern is to protect the investment and ensure the reliability for the client. As engineers, it is our duty to carry out these goals.

Problem Statement

During the design phase of a structural steel support system with a reinforced slab in the province of Veraguas, Panama, the team identified a concern related to the region’s seismic activity. Earthquakes can generate strong inertia forces, uneven deformations, and high stresses that may compromise the performance of the structure. To address this risk, the system requires a detailed analysis to validate its capacity and confirm that it can safely withstand seismic conditions.

Objetives

To perform a modal analysis of the steel structure under free vibration conditions to determine its theoretical vibration modes and validate them through computational simulation in Autodesk Inventor. Furthermore, to evaluate the dynamic response of the structure under seismic excitation represented by earthquake ground motion amplitudes in order to assess its preliminary structural integrity and reliability.

  • Perform a modal analysis
  • Validate the theoretical vibration modes
  • Simulate seismic excitation scenarios
  • Quantify displacement, acceleration, and stress responses
  • Compare theoretical predictions with simulation outcomes

By reducing unnecessary details, we improve mesh quality, avoid over-constraining the system, and ensure a more stable and reliable finite-element analysis.

Conclusion

the results provide a comprehensive understanding of the structural dynamic behavior under both theoretical and simulated conditions. By simulating seismic excitation scenarios, it was possible to observe how the structure responds to real-world dynamic loads, making it clear how displacement, acceleration, and stress distributions evolve during such events. The comparison between analytical expectations and numerical simulations demonstrated strong agreement, confirming the reliability of the modeling approach and the assumptions used.

Design and Development of a RoboCup
Small Size League - Division B Robot

Introduction

Within the framework of the development of technologies applied to robotics and artificial intelligence, the challenge arises to design and build a robot capable of competing in the Small Size League Division B, a category that is part of the robotic soccer competitions organized by RoboCup. The main objective of this project is to combine mechanical design, programming, and electronics skills to create an efficient, fast, and strategically intelligent robot capable of interacting with a team and performing in a competitive environment.

Problem

The SSL Robocup features the competition format of 11v11 football matches with an orange golf ball referred to as the A division, while the 6v6 matches refer to its B division. Teams build robots based on a set of defined constraints and rules.
Division B was chosen according to its own criteria of design and the resources available at that time.
The principal constraints of Division B were defined by the Rules of the RoboCup Small Size League document.

Objetives

General

The purpose of this project is to apply engineering knowledge across programming, mechanical, and electronic fields to the development of an autonomous robot capable of executing movements and actions characteristic of football.

Specific

  • Design each of the actuator elements to perform kicking, dribbling and movement.
  • Mathematical approaches for the prediction of spherics and trajectory planning.
  • Apply image processing techniques for spherical, distance, and velocity detection of moving objects.
  • Design electronic systems for dribbler activation, kick actuators and motion activation.
  • Implement additive manufacturing techniques for the manufacture of parts.
  • Design logical and mathematical programming processes using ROS1 for communication to implement algorithms in parallel.
  • Use of Raspberry Pi 4 controller to communicate actuators and programming algorithms.
