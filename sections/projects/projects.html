Dynamic Seismic Validation of a Diesel Engine Support Structure

Introduction

This Dynamic analysis is part of the design of the structural steel support system with a reinforced slab to support more than two industrial motors by Caterpillar. These motors are responsible for generating electricity for an industrial plant in the province of Veraguas, Panama. The purpose is to evaluate the integrity of the structure under vibrations induced by a natural phenomenon such as an earthquake with a magnitude of 8 on the Richter scale, since that region has been affected more than once over the years by earthquakes of this magnitude. The growing concern is to protect the investment and ensure the reliability for the client. As engineers, it is our duty to carry out these goals.

Problem Statement

During the design phase of a structural steel support system with a reinforced slab in the province of Veraguas, Panama, the team identified a concern related to the region’s seismic activity. Earthquakes can generate strong inertia forces, uneven deformations, and high stresses that may compromise the performance of the structure. To address this risk, the system requires a detailed analysis to validate its capacity and confirm that it can safely withstand seismic conditions.

Objetives

To perform a modal analysis of the steel structure under free vibration conditions to determine its theoretical vibration modes and validate them through computational simulation in Autodesk Inventor. Furthermore, to evaluate the dynamic response of the structure under seismic excitation represented by earthquake ground motion amplitudes in order to assess its preliminary structural integrity and reliability.

  • Perform a modal analysis
  • Validate the theoretical vibration modes
  • Simulate seismic excitation scenarios
  • Quantify displacement, acceleration, and stress responses
  • Compare theoretical predictions with simulation outcomes

Design and Development of a Autonomous Robot
Small Size League - Division B Robot

Introduction

Within the framework of the development of technologies applied to robotics and artificial intelligence, the challenge arises to design and build a robot capable of competing in the Small Size League Division B, a category that is part of the robotic soccer competitions organized by RoboCup. The main objective of this project is to combine mechanical design, programming, and electronics skills to create an efficient, fast, and strategically intelligent robot capable of interacting with a team and performing in a competitive environment.

Problem

The SSL Robocup features the competition format of 11v11 football matches with an orange golf ball referred to as the A division, while the 6v6 matches refer to its B division. Teams build robots based on a set of defined constraints and rules.
Division B was chosen according to its own criteria of design and the resources available at that time.
The principal constraints of Division B were defined by the Rules of the RoboCup Small Size League document.

Objetives

General

The purpose of this project is to apply engineering knowledge across programming, mechanical, and electronic fields to the development of an autonomous robot capable of executing movements and actions characteristic of football.

Specific

  • Design each of the actuator elements to perform kicking, dribbling and movement.
  • Mathematical approaches for the prediction of spherics and trajectory planning.
  • Apply image processing techniques for spherical, distance, and velocity detection of moving objects.
  • Design electronic systems for dribbler activation, kick actuators and motion activation.
  • Implement additive manufacturing techniques for the manufacture of parts.
  • Design logical and mathematical programming processes using ROS1 for communication to implement algorithms in parallel.
  • Use of Raspberry Pi 4 controller to communicate actuators and programming algorithms.

Approach

WBS is organized by critical subsystems that enable modular integration of the robot:

1. Locomotion and Propulsion System

This subsystem governs the robot's dynamic mobility. In SSL, omnidirectional traction is the standard architecture.

  • 1.1 Omnidirectional Wheel Assembly: Design and selection of sub-rollers and wheel hubs to minimize friction and slip.
  • 1.2 Actuation and Transmission: Selection of high-torque BLDC (Brushless DC) motors and optimized gear ratio calculations.
  • 1.3 Motor Mounting and Alignment: Mechanical housing designed to maintain a low center of gravity while ensuring structural rigidity.

    • 2. Ball Handling Mechanisms

    These systems define the robot’s interactive capabilities with the game element.

  • 2.1 Dribbling Unit: An active roller mechanism, utilizing high-friction elastomers, to impart backspin for ball retention.
  • 2.2 Kick/Discharge System:
    • Flat Kicker: A plunger mechanism for high-velocity ground-level passes.
    • Chip Kicker: An angled wedge or lever for parabolic trajectories to bypass opposing robots
  • 2.3 Discharge Electronics: High-voltage Boost Converters and Capacitor Discharge Units (CDUs) to power the solenoids.

    • 3. Electronics and Instrumentation

    The embedded architecture responsible for data processing and power distribution.

  • 3.1 Central Processing Unit (CPU): High-performance FPGA or Microcontroller (MCU) for real-time control execution.
  • 3.2 Electronic Speed Controllers (ESC): Dedicated H-bridges or drivers for precise motor velocity and position control.
  • 3.3 Sensor Suite:
    • Encoders: High-resolution magnetic or optical sensors for odometry feedback.
    • Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU): Gyroscopes and accelerometers for orientation correction.
    • Ball Detection: Infrared (IR) break-beam sensors to confirm ball possession.
  • 3.4 Power Management System: Battery protection circuits (LiPo) and multi-stage voltage regulation (e.g., 3.3V, 5V, 12V).

    • Conclusion

    The project has progressed from a basic functional unit to a scalable architecture. The integration of optimized image processing and structural integrity provides the necessary framework for professional autonomous strategic execution.

    Renewable Energy Innovation

    VortexGen-VIV: Magnetic Resonance Wind Turbine

    Conventional wind turbines face high maintenance costs and structural fatigue due to rotating blades. VortexGen-VIV disrupts the market with a bladeless design based on the Vortex Shedding phenomenon, harnessing oscillating energy for a simpler, more cost-effective, and compact power generation.

    We developed a high-fidelity linear dynamic model integrating fluid and solid mechanics fundamentals. This architecture allows for accurate prediction of mast displacement and oscillation frequency, providing a reliable approximation for real-world implementations in urban environments.

    Using advanced computational tools, we validated that the synchronization between vortex frequencies and the mast's natural frequency maximizes energy capture. Results show a controlled maximum amplitude of 0.07 mm, ensuring structural stability and consistent efficiency.

    The system leverages transverse lift forces generated by the vortex wake. Unlike traditional models, we incorporated the static lift coefficient into the analysis, enabling precise quantification of power output based on local wind velocity profiles.

    Engineered for real-world conditions, the system was validated using meteorological data from Panama City (average speed of 6.25 m/s). With a 1-meter mast and a mass of 7.63 kg, the prototype offers a scalable solution for residential rooftops and dense urban areas.

    Value Proposition

    VortexGen-VIV is more than a theoretical model; it is a robust foundation for the next generation of wind energy. Its simplicity drastically reduces the data required for design decision-making and site selection, offering a viable and efficient alternative for the global energy transition.
